About

Sergio A. Gonzalez (él/he/him), Ph.D., is a tenure-track Assistant Professor of Higher Education specializing in Critical Qualitative Methods in the Department of Educational Foundations, Organizations, and Policy at the University of Pittsburgh. He is an affiliate faculty member in the Gender, Sexuality, and Women's Studies Program and the Cultural Studies Program, serves as the School of Education Ombudsperson, and co-coordinates the EFOP Ph.D. program.

His research has three intersecting commitments: understanding the lived experiences of queer and trans people of color, particularly queer and trans Latine/x communities, in educational settings; examining the role of Jotería Microaffirmations in higher education; and working with co-creators to make meaning of Queer of Color theorizing, such as Jotería Pedagogy, through critical qualitative approaches like pláticas, queer pláticas, and counter-storytelling. This interdisciplinary work draws on Women of Color feminisms, including Black and Queer feminist thought, Chicana/Latina feminisms, and Jotería Studies.

He serves on the editorial board of the Journal of College Student Development and on the national board of the Association for Jotería Arts, Activism, and Scholarship.

Before joining Pitt in 2024, he was an assistant professor at Duquesne University, where he directed the Equity Education Certificate Program, and a visiting professor of Feminist, Gender, and Sexuality Studies at Scripps College. His path to the professoriate began in college access and student affairs, including work with College Track and the LGBTQ+ resource centers at the University of Southern California and the Claremont Colleges.

A first-generation academic and the first in his family to graduate from high school, he carries those experiences into how he shows up in community and navigates academia. He is an hijo to his Madre y Padre and a brother, Tío, Madrina, Nino, and Nieto to his chosen family and family.

        Teaching and mentoring

        His teaching centers co-creation, reflexivity, and community, drawing on critical qualitative inquiry, Chicana/Latina feminisms, and Jotería pedagogy. Graduate courses at the University of Pittsburgh include:

        • Social Justice in Higher Education
        • Contemporary Issues in Racialization, Culture, and Identity in U.S. Higher Education
        • Gender, Sexuality, and Women's Studies in Higher Education
        • Higher Education Administration
        • Advising and Support Skills for Higher Education Professionals
        • Education and Society

        He chairs and serves on dissertation committees, advises master's, Ed.D., and Ph.D. students, and co-coordinates the EFOP Ph.D. program. He has given nearly 40 invited guest lectures, at institutions including UC Berkeley, Rutgers University, the University of Pennsylvania, and the University of Minnesota, along with more than 40 invited keynotes, talks, and workshops.
